Daniele Re, PhD

Signal processing, machine learning & AI engineering for physiological and neural sensing

Algorithm scientist & AI engineer · wearable & neural sensing

ML Consultant, X-trodes · Co-founder, Telephos Intelligence

From modelling binocular vision for a humanoid robot to decoding eye movements from electrodes on the face, my path has followed one question: how do you turn a weak, noisy signal from the body into a measurement you can trust?

I trained as a biomedical and neuro-engineer at the University of Genoa, then did a PhD in cognitive neuroscience at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, showing that attention samples the visual world rhythmically.

As a postdoc at Tel Aviv University I built real-time closed-loop systems on wearable EMG and accelerometer streams and planned non-invasive retinal stimulation. Since then I've worked on wearable biosignal ML as a consultant to X-trodes and co-founded Telephos, where I built a multimodal capture platform for health measurement and the agentic AI service that analyses its data, from the FastAPI backend to deployment.

Across all of it the loop is the same: design how the data is collected, understand the noise, build the model, and validate it against something trustworthy, including reporting what doesn't work.

Telephos Intelligence · co-founder · 2025–now

An agentic AI service for clinical-trial data

I built and deployed the platform end to end: a FastAPI backend with authentication, audit logging and Postgres behind a React front end, and an LLM layer on the Claude API. A tool-calling assistant fetches only the data windows it needs and streams its reasoning live; other features turn plain language into charts, map incoming datasets onto our schema, and flag data-quality problems for human review.

Checked, not assumed: a gold-set evaluation scored by an LLM judge, tracing and cost tracking, versioned prompts with rollback, and 549 automated tests. Deployed with Docker; written with Claude Code, every change reviewed and tested.

X-trodes · consulting · 2024–now

Eye movements from electro-oculography on dry-electrode wearables

PyTorch transformer models that recover eye movements from EOG recorded in everyday conditions, plus the pipelines that make those recordings usable: filtering, artefact rejection, stream alignment, drift correction, signal-quality scoring.

Validated against research-grade reference instruments, with failure regimes mapped under motion, electrode noise, dropped samples and device-to-device variation.

Tel Aviv University · postdoc · 2023–2025

Real-time closed-loop sensing and non-invasive retinal stimulation

A real-time system that detected movement events from wearable EMG and accelerometer streams and triggered an intervention within the movement window. Camera pipelines for gaze, vergence, microsaccades, pupillometry and blinks. SimNIBS electric-field simulations to plan transorbital tACS and temporal-interference stimulation.

Outcome: stimulation plans used in real sessions with participants; first-author paper at IEEE EMBC 2025.

Independent research · simulation · 2026

Focusing ultrasound through the skull

A 2-D acoustic simulation (500 kHz, 64-element array) aiming at a deep, cingulate-like target through five synthetic skulls, comparing three ways of planning the array: ignoring the skull, straight-ray planning, and full-wave time reversal.

Finding: ignoring the skull lands 0.38× the achievable pressure, up to 18 mm off target. Straight rays recover 0.67×, and realistic skull-model errors cost under 0.03 on top, so the planner, not the skull model, is the bottleneck: the case for a fast learned full-wave surrogate.

Hebrew University · PhD · 2017–2023

Attention samples the world rhythmically

Does attention process several objects at once, or sample them in alternation? Dense behavioural time courses, spectral analysis, and careful statistics for periodicity claims, including a published reply to a methodological critique.

Published in Current Biology (2019) and Trends in Cognitive Sciences (2026).

Machine learning

PyTorch transformer and sequence models for physiological time series, transfer from pretrained encoders, Kalman filtering and state-space models, Bayesian models in Stan, classical ML for small samples.

Validation & experiments
